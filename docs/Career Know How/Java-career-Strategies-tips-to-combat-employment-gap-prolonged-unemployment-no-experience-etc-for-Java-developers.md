# Java career   Strategies & tips to combat employment gap, prolonged unemployment, no experience, etc for Java developers

This is a very demotivating situation to be in, and here are 4 things you can do to improve your chances of getting a foot in the door . You need to have both your
Job hunting skills and technical skills to be refined to increase your chances.
Demonstrate your passion for your chosen profession as a Java developer NOT desperation.
How do you demonstrate passion and commitment for your pr ofession as a Java developer? demonstrate your initiatives during that period via self-
taught tutorials and projects, any open-source contribution, any voluntary work like building a website for a charity or a community or ganization, any
certification, any training, etc
Brushing up on the fundamentals so that when you get that 1 in 50 or 1 in 100 opportunity to attend an interview , you can really impress your interviewers
to get a foot in the door .
How do I get that 1 in 50 or 1 in 100 chance to pr ove myself again? You need to look for non traditional avenues for your job hunting. The traditional
avenue is responding to advertisements. This is highly competitive as most applicants respond to advertisements. The non traditional or hidden job
market involves a) cold calling where you get a list of employees you like to work for , especially smaller or ganizations, and then calling them directly ,
b) networking by attending Java User Groups, going to Java trade shows, events, etc c) trying via Java forums by Googling “forum Java jobs W anted”,
etc d) trying for voluntary work say 2-3 days a week whilst applying for paid jobs to get much needed experience to be added to your CV .
Tips that can make a difference
1) Even if you had 1-3 year experience before your employment gap, be prepared to get an entry level job to get a foot back in the door . You can always fast-
track it after a year or so.
2) When you work on self-taught Java projects to gather much needed experience and skills, create a GitHub account and check-in your code. Provide a link to
your code repository in your resume. This proves 3 key things 1) you have the passion and commitment to take some initiatives 2) you have some hands-on
experience 3) improves your chances at the job interview .
3) If you are someone who lacks confidence and good communication skills, then you must work on improving both. Checkout for Toastmasters and take part in
other group sharing. Soft skills like communication is very important to land a good job.
4) Don’ t be afraid to apply for positions seeking 3+ years experience when you only have 1 year experience. Job hunting is a numbers game and the more
applications you send and dif ferent avenues you try , the better chance you will have.
5) If you are not confident about your resume writing or job hunting skills, invest in seeking professional help. There are good books and blogs that will keep
you informed and motivated during your job hunting.
6) Follow up on your applications via phone calls.
7) Don’ t get too hung up on certifications. If you need to have one, just get one. No more. Experience , good resume, good communication skills, good
interviewing skills, good resume writing skills, and good networking skills are going to make the real differ ence in finding work.
8) Don’ t under estimate the power of networking. When send your CV via a networked resource, it gets a dif ferent scrutiny .

9) You need to act on these strategies and tips. There is no other magic bullet. If you don’ t change your approach, things won’ t change for you. T ry the
“Learn –> Apply –> Let the world Know ” approach.
Pick a sought-after technology/framework/tool to learn , apply what you learn via tutorials,self-taught projects, open-source projects, and voluntary work –>
Let the world know by adding your experience to your resume and sharing/marketing via blogging, online portfolio, Linked in profile, and social media.
So, job hunting is a full time job itself where you can allocate time to
1) Apply for paid jobs every day via traditional and non-traditional channels.
2) Apply for voluntary work to gain hands-on experience for say 2-3 days a week. Add this experience to your resume.
3) W ork on self-taught tutorials and projects and add that to your resume when you are confident. Provide a link to your code repository on GitHub.
4) Contribute to open-source projects and add this to your resume.
5) cold call and network.
6) brush up on core Java, JEE, and sought-after frameworks fundamentals so that you can do well when you get an opportunity to interview .
7) improve your job hunting, resume writing, networking and interviewing skills.
8) You will start to gain more confidence with time.
9) Google is your best friend to do some research everyday .
2 key take away points are “GIVE” and “MARKET“
Time to time I get emails to the ef fect of
“I have been trying to GET a Java job for the last 6 months, but no luck”
My initial reaction is – What have you acquired in terms of experience and skills to GIVE? .
1) Can you GIVE at least 3 months of hands-on experience in sought-after technologies/frameworks acquired through tutorials, self-taught projects, opensource
contributions, or voluntary work.
2) How good are you with your Java/JEE fundamentals? Can you GIVE a good performance in the job interviews?
So, Give what your prospective employers want before GETTING what you want.
Once you know what to “GIVE”, you need to MARKET them via good resume, good interview performance, and networking (both online and in person).
Recruiters do frequent sites like LinkedIn.com, Java Forums, and other Java specific sites.
